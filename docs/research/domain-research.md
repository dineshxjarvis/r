# Strata — Research Brief: Verified Bottlenecks, Missing Pieces, and Niche Datasets for SIH 2025 PS 26024

**Bottom line:** All three gaps your thesis targets — the missing obligation layer, the absent shared cross-body defect register, and weak field-evidence integrity — are confirmed by primary sources, but the government's own newly launched **National Coal Mines Safety Report (NCMSR) Portal (2024)** now partly occupies the accident-reporting and safety-audit space, so your novelty must be argued *against* it (atomic machine-checkable obligations + deduplicated cross-body register + anti-spoofing evidence), and your strongest and rarest competitive assets are the **RegNLP/ObliQA obligation-extraction corpora** and **MSHA's record-level inspection→violation→penalty open data**, which almost no competing team will find or use.

## TL;DR
- **Bottlenecks verified.** DGMS is chronically under-staffed (167 sanctioned technical/inspecting posts, 15–20% vacant); the CAG found EC-condition post-clearance monitoring essentially unenforced ("No penalty has been imposed on even a single violator in the last two years"); the six-layer CIL inspection pyramid is officially documented but has **no consolidated cross-body defect register**. The obligation-layer and shared-register gaps are real and defensible; the evidence-integrity gap is real but partly being closed by NCMSR — position against it.
- **Rarest competitive edge.** The **RegNLP/ObliQA family** (27,869 obligation-grounded QA pairs; an ObligationClassifier) and the **REGOBLIGATION/GAPBENCH deontic datasets** are purpose-built for exactly your obligation layer and evidence-to-obligation matching. Paired with **MSHA's ~20-file open enforcement data** — the only large public dataset linking an inspection event to violations, penalties and appeal outcomes — you have a genuine, defensible model-training story.
- **Be realistic about data.** Most non-US regulators (South Africa SAMRASS, Chile SERNAGEOMIN, WA DMIRS, Poland WUG, NSW) publish only **aggregate PDFs, not record-level open data**; India's richest signals (NGT orders, PARIVESH EC letters, Star Rating PDFs) exist but are **semi-structured and scattered** — which is both the opportunity and the labour cost of the project.

## Key Findings

### Part 1 — Bottlenecks: what's verified, what's overstated

**1. DGMS staffing shortage — VERIFIED.** The official DGMS statement (DGMS FAQ, mirrored on dgms.net and geocities.ws/minsafety/faq.htm) reads verbatim: *"The DGMS has a total sanctioned strength of 167 technical officers/Inspecting officers at different levels. Of these, 15 to 20% of posts remain vacant at any given time due to time lag in recruitment etc."* The same FAQ concedes the operational consequence: *"it is humanly impossible for them to ensure compliance by management at all times and at all work places."* This is reinforced at the top of the political system: the **Parliamentary Standing Committee on Labour, Textiles and Skill Development's Fifteenth Report (Demands for Grants 2026-27)**, presented in the Lok Sabha in March 2026 by chairperson Basavaraj Bommai, "urged [the Ministry] to expedite the filling of vacancies in the Directorate General of Mines Safety (DGMS) and adopt modern monitoring technologies to prevent accidents."
- **Overstated/unverifiable:** the **mines-per-inspector ratio** and the **percentage of mines inspected annually** are **NOT published as official figures**. DGMS describes its inspections as "periodic … to make sample checks," not full coverage. Present any ratio strictly as your own labelled computation: ~167 sanctioned posts, ~15–20% vacant (≈134–142 in position), against 408 coal-producing mines (Ministry of Coal, as on 31 March 2024) plus ~2,500 metalliferous mines filing returns (est. ~6,000 total) and ~29 oil projects — i.e. tens of mines per officer.

**2. Six-layer inspection pyramid — VERIFIED and documented; duplication implied.** Ministry of Coal *Safety in Coal Mines* annual-report chapters (e.g. coal.gov.in/…/chap11AnnualReport1819en.pdf; Chapter11-en.pdf 2019-20) lay out the exact cadence: mine-level Safety Officer and Workmen's Inspector; **monthly** Pit Safety Committee inspection; **quarterly** Inter-Unit Safety Assessment (IUSA); **six-monthly** Central Safety Council inspection; multidisciplinary **Inter-Area Safety Audit** on a "specifically designed audit format developed by Corporate Safety Division"; and a **Check Audit** that exists "to assess the quality of overall audit procedures as well as find out lacunas … during said mine safety audit process." No consolidated register across these bodies is described — the very existence of the Check Audit is evidence that CIL itself sees audit quality and observation-closure as unresolved. The apex body is the **Internal Safety Organisation (ISO)** in each subsidiary (coalindia.in/departments/safety-rescue).

**3. EC six-monthly compliance monitoring — VERIFIED as a major, quotable failure.** Under the EIA Notification 2006 and MoEFCC O.M. J-11013/41/2006-IA II(I) dated 6 April 2011, six-monthly EC compliance reports must go to the MoEFCC Regional Office (by 1 June and 1 December) and be displayed on the company website. The **CAG performance audit "Environmental Clearance and Post Clearance Monitoring" (Report No. 39 of 2016, tabled in Parliament on 10 March 2017)**, covering 4,534 projects approved 2008–2015, found the system near-collapse: RO Environment Wings were grossly understaffed (only one scientist in position against four sanctioned in Bengaluru, Chandigarh and Dehradun, and five in Shillong), proponents routinely failed to upload reports, and — the killer quote — *"No penalty has been imposed on even a single violator in the last two years."* (CAG Chapter 7, cag.gov.in; corroborated by Mongabay India and Hindustan Times, 11 Mar 2017). MoEFCC's June 2022 EC compliance module (Down To Earth) streamlines *submission* but does not atomise conditions — leaving your obligation layer unaddressed.

**4. CMR 2017 Regulation 40(3) electronic attendance — VERIFIED as legally permitted, NOT yet a documented approved deployment.** The regulation text (Indian Kanoon doc/3531525; CMPDI clearinghouse CMR gazette PDF) explicitly provides that *"electronic punching or registry system as approved by the Chief Inspector may also be provided and used for the purpose of identification, marking attendance and recording the name of the person"* alongside a hard copy. RFID/GPS is deployed for **vehicles** (PIB 1810551: SCCL Vehicle Tracking System with RFID tags + GPS in all mines; CIL/MCL CCTV + GPS-VTS), and smart cap-lamps with RFID/Wi-Fi tags are commercially available (MineARC, NLT Digital, MST Global). **I found no primary-source evidence of a Chief-Inspector-approved electronic *personnel-attendance* system in an Indian coal mine** — a genuine open regulatory gap and a clean legal basis for your attendance/evidence module.

**5. CIL vs DGMS accident-statistics reconciliation — VERIFIED as a scope/timing gap, not a scandal.** Every CIL accident table is footnoted *"Subject to reconciliation with DGMS & Accident Statistics are maintained calendar year-wise in conformity with DGMS practice."* The difference is **scope**: CIL counts only its eight subsidiaries; DGMS compiles all-India coal+lignite (CIL + SCCL + NLCIL + captive/commercial private + illegal). Illustration: CIL's own 2022 figure was 20 fatalities, while the DGMS-compiled **coal-and-lignite** death toll for 2022 was 28 (Rajya Sabha Unstarred Q. 1866, answered 17 March 2025; data.gov.in dataset "year-wise total number of deaths … coal and lignite mines"). **Do not overclaim this as data manipulation.**
- **Latest DGMS counts (label definitions precisely):** DGMS's own account (@DGMS1902) reports **coal-mine** figures of 38 fatal accidents in 2023 and *"38 fatal accidents in coal mines (40 fatalities)"* in 2024. The DGMS-compiled **coal+lignite deaths** series (Rajya Sabha) is 53 (2020), 51 (2021), 28 (2022), 41 (2023), 53 provisional (2024). FACTLY's tabulation of DGMS **fatal accidents in coal**: 48 (2020), 43 (2021), 24 (2022), 38 (2023), 38 (2024).

**6. Contractor safety — VERIFIED as the dominant risk with no cross-mine tracking.** Per Mining-Technology.com citing DGMS, *"contract-based workers are 2.47 times more likely to suffer from an accident at work than full-time workers"* and *"Around 100,000 people are employed as contract workers in Indian mines"*; DGMS Deputy Director General Sanjibon Ray cited *"lack of adequate training and safety awareness of the contractual workforce."* The Lalmatia/ECL 2016 disaster (British Safety Council India) illustrates contractor non-compliance and weak CIL inspection follow-through. **No system tracks a contractor's safety history across mines** — a clean, high-value gap for Strata.

### Part 2 — Missing pieces
- **NCMSR Portal (launched 2024, live) — the biggest threat to your novelty.** The Ministry of Coal has already launched the **National Coal Mines Safety Report Portal** with two modules: an **Accident Module** (near-time reporting within 24 hours) and a **Safety Audit Module**, developed under the **High-Level Expert Committee on Safety in Coal Mines**; it "monitors actions based on recommendations from various inquiries" (PIB 2048428; Ministry of Coal Annual Report 2025-26, chap14). Secretary Amrit Lal Meena has pushed "100% safety audits across all coal mines." **You must differentiate explicitly** — NCMSR does accident reporting and audit tracking, not obligation atomisation, cross-body deduplication, or evidence integrity.
- **SMP / risk-assessment framework — VERIFIED, digitisable.** CMR 2017 Reg. 104 mandates a Safety Management Plan per mine (identify hazards, assess risks, record, make available). The Coal Controller's Organisation ("Safety and Health Monitoring System Audit," coalcontroller.gov.in) states plainly: *"One of the reasons for the ineffective implementation of the present system of risk-based safety management in Indian coal mines is the lack of suitable mechanisms or processes for auditing the SMPs at regular intervals."* A direct opening for structured obligation representation.
- **Machine-readable obligations — essentially absent in India.** No Indian statutory-obligation knowledge base exists; PARIVESH/EC modules handle submission, not atomisation.
- **DGMS circulars corpus — scattered, not bulk-downloadable.** Circulars live on dgms.gov.in (UserView/index?mid=1313) and mirror dgms.net; no single bulk index/API found. Plan to scrape per-page.
- **CMPDI digital-twin / drone-LiDAR — VERIFIED, real programme.** CMPDI operates survey-grade drones (LiDAR + optical + thermal) procured 2021 for volumetric measurement of in-situ excavation, OB dumps and coal stocks, plus thermal detection of underground fire spots (PSU Watch; Ministry of Coal Annual Report 2024-25 chap3). Geomatics/remote-sensing is ~16.7% of CMPDI revenue (FundsIndia IPO note). Industry-standard drone-LiDAR volumetrics reach 1–3% accuracy. Outputs appear internal; no open standard-format release found.
- **Court of Inquiry / inquiry recommendations — now hosted on NCMSR**, a potential root-cause text corpus; public access model unconfirmed.

### Part 3 — Niche and unusual datasets (highest priority)

## Details — dataset-by-dataset (URL, contents, size, format, licence, access, and why it matters)

### A. Regulatory-NLP / obligation-extraction corpora (serve Gap 1: the obligation layer)
- **RegNLP / ObliQA** — `github.com/RegNLP/ObliQADataset`. **27,869** obligation-grounded QA pairs (train/dev/test ≈ 22k/2.8k/2.7k, plus a 446-question hidden test set) built from **40** Abu Dhabi Global Markets financial-regulation documents (**≈640,000 words**), validated via NLI. The org also ships **ObligationClassifier** (LegalBERT fine-tuned for obligation vs non-obligation), ObliQA-MultiPassage, the RePASs metric, and a full BM25+dense RAG pipeline. Open on GitHub. **The closest public analogue to atomising EC/DGMS/CMR prose into machine-checkable obligations; almost no competing team will find it.** (Source: RIRAG, arXiv:2409.05677, Gokhan et al.)
- **REGOBLIGATION + GAPBENCH** — ComplianceNLP paper, `arxiv.org/pdf/2604.23585`. REGOBLIGATION: **1,847** regulatory sentences from **SEC (712), MiFID II (614), Basel III (521)**, annotated for 23 NER types, four deontic modalities (OBLIGATION/PERMISSION/PROHIBITION/RECOMMENDATION) and cross-references. GAPBENCH: **423** obligation–policy pairs labelled COMPLIANT (210) / PARTIAL GAP (128) / FULL GAP (85), κ=0.81. **GAPBENCH is a direct supervision signal for evidence-to-obligation matching (Gap 2).** ⚠️ The paper marks REGOBLIGATION **"(to be released)"** — treat as aspirational until you confirm a download.
- **DeonticBench** — `arxiv.org/pdf/2606.05009`. Deontic-reasoning tasks (SARA tax statutes numeric/binary, airline baggage policy, USCIS-AAO immigration appeals). Useful for statutory-reasoning evaluation of your obligation engine.
- **LexGLUE** — `aclanthology.org/2022.acl-long.297`; Kaggle/HuggingFace mirrors. 7 tasks incl. EUR-LEX (EU-law multi-label), LEDGAR (contract-provision topics), UNFAIR-ToS. Good for pre-training/benchmarking legal encoders. Open.
- **LegalBench** — 162 collaboratively-built legal-reasoning tasks (Guha et al. 2023). Benchmark, US-centric, not domain-specific.
- **PolicyKG** — `arxiv.org/html/2608.09028`. An LLM pipeline that reads a policy PDF, classifies each sentence as obligation/permission/prohibition, lifts it into first-order deontic logic, and emits executable **SHACL** constraints with an audit trail from sentence→formula→shape. **Effectively a published architecture blueprint for your obligation engine** — cite and adapt.
- **CUAD, ContractNLI, MAUD, EURLEX57K, BSARD** (known) — auxiliaries for clause extraction, directly relevant to contractor Form XIII parsing.

### B. Record-level mine-safety regulator data (serve Gap 2: shared defect register; inspection→violation model)
- **MSHA Open Government Data** — `arlweb.msha.gov/opengovernmentdata/ogimsha.asp`. **~20 pipe-delimited (|) flat files**, header row, data from 1/1/2000, refreshed **weekly (Fridays)**, no authentication, full bulk download (.zip + .txt definition files). Files: Mines, Inspections, Violations, AssessedViolations, 107(a) OrdersIssued, Accidents/Injuries (Form 7000-1), ProdQuarterly/Yearly, ContractorProd, ControllerOperatorHistory, Conferences, ContestedViolations, CivilPenaltyDocketsDecisions, and health samples (CoalDust, Quartz, Noise, Area, PersonalHealth), AddressOfRecord. Interactive front-end: Mine Data Retrieval System (msha.gov/data-and-reports/data-sources-and-calculators). **The only large public dataset linking an inspection event → specific violations (with the "conditions or practices which caused … a violation") → assessed penalties → contest/appeal outcome.** Your gold-standard training corpus for a defect-register / violation-severity model and a live proof-of-concept of the shared-register idea. Globally underused.
- **Queensland RSHQ** — `data.qld.gov.au/dataset/high-potential-incidents` and `…/quarterly-mines-and-quarries-safety-statistics-data` (also on data.gov.au). CC-BY-4.0, XLS, downloadable. HPIs categorised by hazard/incident type/major equipment/worksite; quarterly frequency rates (serious accidents, HPI, recordable/lost-time injuries) from July 2012; worker numbers by mine and sector. Excellent leading-indicator taxonomy. Contact: dataanalytics@rshq.qld.gov.au.
- **Poland WUG** — `wug.gov.pl/bhp/statystyki_wypadkow`. Annual accident and dangerous-occurrence statistics 2019–2026, plus the annual "Stan bezpieczeństwa i higieny pracy w górnictwie." Mostly aggregate HTML/PDF, Polish.
- **South Africa SAMRASS (DMPR, formerly DMRE)** — `dmpr.gov.za/…/Occupational-Safety-in-Mining` with SAMRASS Codebook (PDF) + report forms SAMRASS 1–9 (PDF/DOCX); aggregate fatality/injury tables only (e.g. 2023: 56 fatalities/2,184 injuries). **NOT record-level open microdata** — but the codebook itself is a ready-made accident-classification schema you can reuse.
- **Chile SERNAGEOMIN** — `sernageomin.cl/accidentabilidad-minera` and `repositorio.sernageomin.cl`. Annual/semi-annual "Estadísticas de Accidentabilidad Minera" PDFs with **fatal-accident case narratives by company** (Spanish); aggregate, not open microdata — but the narratives are usable as an NLP accident-narrative corpus.
- **Western Australia DMIRS/DMPE** — `dasc.dmirs.wa.gov.au`, `catalogue.data.wa.gov.au` (MINEDEX dataset, CC-BY-NC, daily-updated). Spatial/geoscience + operational/inspection metadata, **NOT safety-incident microdata**; incidents flow through the SRS portal, public output aggregate.
- **NSW Resources Regulator** — `resources.nsw.gov.au` safety performance overviews (PDF, aggregate).
- **Verdict:** MSHA is the only true record-level open enforcement dataset; every other regulator is aggregate. State this in your comparative governance framing — it is itself a finding, and it explains why an India-first structured register would be novel.

### C. Satellite / geospatial datasets (serve Gap 3: field-evidence integrity, change detection)
- **Maus et al. Global Mining Polygons v2** — `doi.org/10.1594/PANGAEA.942325`. **44,929** polygon features covering **101,583 km²**; GeoPackage (WGS84) + per-country CSV + grid rasters (30 arc-sec / 5 arc-min / 30 arc-min). Accuracy: overall 88.3%, Kappa 0.77, F1 0.87. **CC-BY-4.0**, open; also on Google Earth Engine (`projects/sat-io/open-datasets/global-mining`) and fineprint.global/viewer. (v1 = 21,060 polygons / 57,277 km² at doi.org/10.1594/PANGAEA.910894.) Ground-truth mine-extent baseline for detecting unauthorised expansion vs EC lease boundaries.
- **Global Energy Monitor — Global Coal Mine Tracker** — `globalenergymonitor.org/projects/global-coal-mine-tracker`. Operating/proposed/closed mines, production, methane, ownership chains, and a "Coal Mine Boundaries & Methane Sources" supplement; per-mine GEM.wiki pages. **CC-BY-4.0**, XLS via an email-gated download form. Strong India coverage. Latest release Aug 2026.
- **CartoDEM / Bhoonidhi / Bhuvan** and **CPCB CAAQMS / OpenAQ** (known) — terrain and air-quality corroboration layers for the evidence engine.

### D. Obscure Indian datasets (unique-to-India edge)
- **NCMSR Portal** (Ministry of Coal) — accident + safety-audit modules (Part 2). Access model unconfirmed; walk through it during Week 1.
- **Star Rating of Coal Mines** — `starrating.coal.gov.in`. Result PDFs; 50 opencast / 47 underground parameters across 7 modules — a ready obligation/parameter schema you can convert straight into structured records.
- **PARIVESH / environmentclearance.nic.in** — EC letters (`Auth/openletter.aspx?EC=<integer>`) and EAC minutes (`/writereaddata/Form-1A/Minutes/`). Semi-structured PDFs; your primary EC-condition training source for obligation atomisation.
- **NGT orders on coal mining** — a corpus of adjudicated **non-compliance findings** (via the NGT site / IndiaKanoon). High-value negative-label supervision for evidence-to-obligation matching; confirm scrape feasibility.
- **Coal Controller returns, SWCS/PRIMS, eShram, Shram Suvidha inspection data, IBM Mining Tenement System, NGDR, SPCB OCEMS/CPCB continuous emission monitoring** (known) — mostly login-gated or aggregate; do not assume bulk access.

### E. Near-miss / leading-indicator datasets (auxiliary)
- **CPWR SC-SMIS** — `cpwr.com/research/…/the-safety-climate-safety-management-information-system-sc-smis`. Free safety-climate management system with 8 leading indicators + policy/template repository. Cross-industry leading-indicator template.
- **Queensland HPI** (above) doubles as a near-miss/high-potential-incident taxonomy.

## Recommendations
1. **Weeks 1–2 — lock the moat against NCMSR.** Immediately (a) download the MSHA 20-file set and the ObliQA/ObligationClassifier repo, and (b) do a hands-on walkthrough of the NCMSR Portal to confirm exactly what its Safety Audit Module already consolidates. Build a working obligation-extraction demo on 20–30 real PARIVESH EC letters + CMR 2017 Reg. 104/40 + one Star Rating parameter sheet. **Deliverable:** a machine-checkable obligation schema (obligation ID, source clause, deontic type, evidence type, frequency, responsible role). **Threshold:** if you cannot atomise ≥80% of a sample EC letter's conditions into structured records, narrow scope to EC + CMR only.
2. **Weeks 3–4 — the shared defect register.** Adopt MSHA's inspection→violation→penalty structure as the schema template and map it onto the six CIL inspection layers to produce one deduplicated register with closure-status tracking. Prototype evidence-to-obligation matching on GAPBENCH/REGOBLIGATION **if released**; if not, hand-label ~200 pairs from NGT orders + EC compliance PDFs as your own supervision set.
3. **Weeks 5–6 — evidence integrity.** Implement geo-tag + timestamp + device-attestation for field photos and cross-check against Maus polygons / CartoDEM change detection. Frame CMR 40(3) electronic attendance as a "Chief-Inspector-approvable" module, citing the regulation text as legal basis.
4. **Weeks 7–8 — differentiation narrative + demo.** Explicitly benchmark Strata against NCMSR, ICIS, SWCS and Khanan Prahari; your unique claims are (a) atomic obligations, (b) cross-body register, (c) anti-spoofing evidence. Anchor the problem statement in CAG Report 39/2016 ("No penalty … even a single violator") and the 2026 Standing Committee on Labour recommendation to fill DGMS vacancies and adopt modern monitoring.
5. **Threshold that changes the plan:** if the NCMSR Safety Audit Module already consolidates cross-body observations, pivot hard toward the obligation layer + evidence integrity — the areas where the government has done least.

## Caveats
- **Mines-per-inspector and %-inspected-annually are NOT official figures** — present only as clearly-labelled computations built on the 167-post / 15–20%-vacant baseline and 408 coal-producing mines.
- The **CIL–DGMS "reconciliation gap" is not a scandal** — it is a scope (CIL-only vs all-India coal+lignite) and timing (provisional vs finalised) difference. State DGMS series definitions precisely: "fatal accidents" ≠ "fatalities" ≠ "coal+lignite deaths."
- **REGOBLIGATION/GAPBENCH are marked "to be released"** — treat as aspirational until you confirm a download; have the hand-labelled NGT/EC fallback ready.
- **NCMSR Portal is live and government-built** — argue novelty against it, not around it.
- **Most international regulator "datasets" are aggregate PDFs** (SAMRASS, SERNAGEOMIN, WUG, NSW, WA) — budget for OCR/manual structuring, not API ingestion. MSHA is the exception.
- **DGMS's "DGMS at a Glance 2023" PDF and several data.gov.in resources are robots-blocked to automated fetch** — plan for manual download; the 167/15–20% figure originates in that material and the DGMS FAQ.
- **Several Indian sources (eShram, Shram Suvidha, IBM Tenement System, NGDR, SPCB OCEMS) are login-gated or aggregate** — do not assume bulk access.
- **No documented Chief-Inspector-approved electronic personnel-attendance deployment** was found in any Indian coal mine — this is an inference from absence of evidence, not a proven negative; verify with a targeted RTI or a DGMS-circular scrape if it becomes central to your pitch.
